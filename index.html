Key Insight · Forward

At the same timestep \(t\), each schedule retains a different amount of original signal. Linear steadily reduces signal under the classic DDPM-style \(\beta\) schedule. Cosine preserves structure longer through a smoother curve. Exponential is configured here as a late-collapse schedule: the image stays readable for longer, then rapidly becomes noise near the end. The schedule choice directly shapes what the model sees and learns at each timestep.

At the same signal retention \(\bar{\alpha}\), each schedule reaches that noise level at a different timestep. Linear is the first to hit any given \(\bar{\alpha}\) — its curve drops fastest mid-schedule. Cosine arrives later and more gradually. Exponential takes the longest to descend, then collapses sharply once it does. So even at matched noise levels, models trained under different schedules see fundamentally different \((x_t, t)\) pairs — schedule choice shapes the per-step training distribution, not just the marginals.

Denoising · Image-to-Image Recovery
\[ x_{t-1} = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}\, \hat{x}_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_{t-1} - \sigma_t^2}\, \hat{\varepsilon} + \sigma_t\, z \]

SDEdit-style reverse: start from the partially-noised \(x_{t^\star}\) and \(\eta\)-DDIM-sample down to \(x_0\). Each step uses the network's noise prediction \(\hat{\varepsilon} = \varepsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\), Tweedie's reconstruction \(\hat{x}_0 = (x_t - \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\hat{\varepsilon}) / \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\), and stochastic noise \(\sigma_t\, z\) with \(z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)\). \(\eta=0 \Rightarrow \sigma_t = 0\) (deterministic DDIM); \(\eta=1\) recovers DDPM marginals.

Key Insight · Denoising

At fixed strength all three schedules start reverse from the same \(t^\star\), but each schedule injected a different noise level into \(x_{t^\star}\) — so the recovery quality you see in Final Quality below is essentially measuring "how much signal does this schedule retain at \(t^\star\)?". Higher \(\bar{\alpha}_{t^\star}\) → easier denoising → better PSNR/SSIM, lower MSE.

At matched signal retention \(\bar{\alpha}\) the model receives an essentially identical noisy input across schedules — the only thing that varies is the \(t^\star\) label fed to the conditioning. Differences in Final Quality here therefore isolate how well the trained denoiser handles each schedule's t-conditioning, not the noise level itself. This is the right comparison for "which schedule is intrinsically easier to denoise from?".
